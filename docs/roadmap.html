Our own way of doing the job

Three ideas the app is built around

The Desk

The home screen answers one question: what needs you today? The next release and what's blocking it, clashes, reports due, renewals and last month's earnings.

The store grid

One page per book, with editions down the side and stores across the top. Each square shows the price, the status and any problem, so a gap stands out at once.

Every row accounted for

Reports are treated as a ledger. Each row is imported, a duplicate, or set aside with a reason, and the total has to match the store's own.

Chosen by you on 6 November 2026

What it is

The business side of being an author: catalog, editions, ISBNs, prices, store links, accounts, release dates, launch plans, marketing and royalties, plus a royalty and pricing calculator.

How sales come in

By importing report files, so it needs no outside accounts.

The first release

The catalog and the money together in 1.0. Catalog first was recommended, because a smaller first release reaches him sooner. To keep the risk down, 1.0 reaches him as two test builds.

Which stores

KDP, Barnes & Noble Press, ACX, Google Play Books, IngramSpark, Draft2Digital, Kobo, Apple Books and Lulu, with a screen for matching columns for any other. The first five are in 1.0 and the rest in 1.1. Google Play Books moved up on 6 November: he self-publishes there, so it's one of his main platforms.

Passwords

A locked vault. No passwords was recommended, because it makes this app a password manager, which needs its own review. See the design.

How far the money goes

Costs, profit per book and series, direct sales, author copies, and a year-end summary. No tax advice.

Marketing

Plans and results. Posting stays with automation and RT Markdown Reader, and Substack and podcast operating notes stay out.

Claude

A connector before leaving Notion, off until you turn it on, with four levels. The vault is never reachable.

What else moves

The business checklist and personal finances. The job search stays out. Formation only was recommended, because personal money isn't an author's business. It's a separate section, off by default, with its own lock.

Bank data

Bank files, plus a feed from his own automation. The app never holds a bank login.

Audiobook tracking

Per title and store: which recording is live, whether its voice is human or synthesized, what rights it has, and which are waiting to be replaced. Google's auto-made audiobooks use a generic voice, and he plans to replace them with versions made in RT Audio Studio. This app tracks that, and RT Audio Studio makes the audio.

Facts to check before or during the build

Real reports

The KDP files on hand are from December 2024, and the other stores' layouts haven't been seen. Step zero collects a recent one from each store. For a store with no direct account yet, such as Kobo, the reader waits for a real file, and Match your columns covers it until then.

ACX rates

Secondary sources say a new royalty model became the default on 26 May 2026, at 50% exclusive and 30% non-exclusive, with the old 40% and 25% ending at the end of 2026. Check this against ACX and the contract for each title.

KDP's ebook band

The owner's own guide says the 70% rate reaches $12.99 since 7 July 2026, citing KDP's pricing page, and some outside sources still say $9.99. KDP's page decides.

How KENP royalties appear

The sample report has pages read but no rows that show a KENP royalty. A report from a month with Kindle Unlimited reads will settle it.

Google Play Books

Notion says he publishes audiobooks and physical books there. Print sales on Google weren't confirmed for this plan, so a real report will show what it covers. A listing can record either route.

Replacing a Google audiobook

Google's help doesn't spell out swapping an auto-narrated book for your own recording, so try it on one book first. Its accepted audio formats and limits go on a rate card, once they're confirmed in Partner Center.

Each recording's rights

Every title's ACX contract has to be read before a recording goes anywhere else.
